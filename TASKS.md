Round-timeout + turn-deadline hard reliability bound — every provider round (top-level or any delegate, isolated or not) is now guaranteed to end, one way or another, well inside a bounded wall-clock window, instead of a single round being able to sit unresponsive indefinitely with the Stop button unable to help. **Implemented 2026-09-22, then substantially reworked the same session after a design flaw was found — see the second entry below.**
  - **Found live**: a real turn (a rich multi-slide presentation) sat completely unresponsive for over an hour with zero visible error — confirmed directly against the running process via Chrome DevTools Protocol (`Runtime.queryObjects`/`getProperties` on every live Promise, `process._getActiveHandles()`/`_getActiveRequests()`, `lsof`) that nothing was actually pending anywhere: no open network connection, no pending Promise in the heap. The round had died silently with no bound on how long the app would just sit there before anyone noticed, and Stop had nothing left to cancel.
  - **User then explicitly tightened the requirement twice, mid-investigation**: "It should not take more than an hour" → "It should create the presentation in less than 15 minutes" — required an actual hard time-bound mechanism, not just a diagnosis.
  - **Two-tier bound**: a per-round timeout (`ROUND_TIMEOUT_MS`, base 5 min) races a real `provider.stream()` call via a combined `AbortSignal` (the real user/turn signal plus a `setTimeout`-driven one) — on firing, it's handled through the exact same "clean abort" path a genuine Stop click already goes through, distinguished only by `timedOut()` so a real Stop still ends the whole turn immediately while a mere round-timeout is treated as one failed attempt and the loop continues. A turn-wide `TURN_DEADLINE_MS` (15 min, `RunState.deadlineAt`, shared and never reset across every nesting level — including an isolated delegate's own fresh round *count*, which is a separate budget from the wall-clock one) stops the whole turn from running long in aggregate even if every individual round stays under its own timeout; once past it, `runLoop` falls into the same round-cap wrap-up path `MAX_TOTAL_ROUNDS` exhaustion already used (one final, tools-disabled call synthesizing a real answer from whatever's already been gathered, rather than a bare stub).
  - **A real mid-implementation design correction, caught by the test suite, not the user**: the first version of the timeout catch block always ended the whole turn on any abort (matching pre-existing Stop-click behavior) — its own new tests then failed against it, revealing that a *timeout* must not end the turn the way a genuine Stop does. Redesigned to `continue` the loop on a timeout instead of returning.
  - **2026-09-22, same day — a second, real design flaw found during the full presentation-pipeline review below, and fixed**: the flat 5-minute timeout applied identically to every retry, and a timed-out round's retry resent the *exact same* prompt/context with zero adaptation — so a round that genuinely needed close to or over 5 minutes (plausible on a slow local model; confirmed elsewhere in this project that a single real round can take several minutes) could never converge: every attempt got killed just before finishing and restarted from zero, burning the entire 15-minute turn deadline on repeated wasted restarts, while surfacing a "stopped automatically, trying again" warning each time that reads as a failure but was actually this mechanism sabotaging itself. This is very likely a major contributor to the user's "fails in between... takes huge amount of time... crapy messages" complaint that prompted the review. **Fixed**: the per-round budget is now adaptive, not flat. `RunState.consecutiveRoundTimeouts` counts how many times in a row the *same* round has just timed out (reset to 0 the moment a round actually completes) — each retry's budget roughly doubles (`ROUND_TIMEOUT_MS * (consecutiveRoundTimeouts + 1)`), bounded above by the real remaining turn budget minus a fixed safety margin (`ROUND_TIMEOUT_SAFETY_MARGIN_MS`, so ending a round never leaves literally zero time for whatever runs next) and below by a floor (`ROUND_TIMEOUT_FLOOR_MS`, so even a round started with almost no turn time left gets a workable window). A round's *first* attempt at anything is unchanged (still the original 5 minutes, so a genuinely hung round is still caught exactly as fast as before) — only a round that's already timed out once gets more room on its retry. The warning message wording also changed, from "This step was taking longer than 5 minutes and was stopped automatically" (reads like a tool failure) to explicitly attributing the wait to the model generating, not a tool being stuck.
